# Security & Privacy

> This vault will hold your child's name, allegations, court documents, and your legal strategy. It is one of the most sensitive collections of information you will ever keep. Five minutes of setup protects all of it.

---

## Protect the Vault Itself

1. **Turn on full-disk encryption** — FileVault (Mac) or BitLocker/Device encryption (Windows). This is the single biggest protection and it's free.
2. **Use a strong login password** on every device that holds the vault, and lock the screen when you walk away.
3. **Think before you sync.** Cloud sync (iCloud, Dropbox, OneDrive, Obsidian Sync) is convenient and gives you a backup — but it puts your case on someone else's servers and on every signed-in device. If you sync:
   - Use a provider account the other party has never had access to
   - Check which devices are signed in and remove old ones
   - Prefer end-to-end encrypted options where available
4. **Back up.** An encrypted external drive kept somewhere safe, or an encrypted cloud backup. Losing the vault before a hearing is the disaster scenario.
5. **Shared computers are not safe places for the vault.** If the other party ever had access to the device or knows your passwords, change the passwords and move the vault.

## Who Can See It

- **Change every password the other party might know** — email first (it's the recovery key to everything else), then the computer, then cloud accounts.
- **Check account access pages** (Google/Apple/Microsoft "devices" and "security" pages) for sessions you don't recognise.
- **Children should not have access to the vault.** Ever. It contains material about their parents that they should never read, and it can compromise your case if repeated.

## Legal Boundaries (Australia)

- **Check publication restrictions before sharing.** Part XIVB of the Family Law Act restricts public communication that identifies people involved in family law proceedings. Limited exceptions and court permissions exist; do not assume they apply to a social-media post or a shared case summary. See the Court’s [privacy guidance](https://www.fcfcoa.gov.au/pubs/court-privacy) and obtain advice about your circumstances.
- **Covert recordings**: laws on recording conversations differ by state. Get advice before making or relying on a recording the other person didn't know about.
- **Obtain documents lawfully.** Accessing the other party's email, phone, or accounts without authorisation is illegal and can destroy your case and expose you to charges — no matter what you find.

## Using AI With Your Case

- Use AI tools under **your own account** on **your own device**.
- Understand what the tool does with your data — check whether your conversations are used for training and turn that off where the option exists.
- Do not assume uploading to an AI service is legally permitted or confidential. Check any court orders, restrictions on use of disclosed documents, provider access and retention settings, and obtain advice where needed. Local storage does not mean cloud analysis stays on your device. See the [Family Law Rules](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L01197/latest/text), including rule 6.04 on use of disclosed documents.

## If You Believe Your Devices Are Compromised

If there's any history of stalking, monitoring, or coercive control:
- Assume shared accounts, family location sharing, and old devices leak information
- Get the device checked before storing your case on it
- The eSafety Commissioner ([esafety.gov.au](https://www.esafety.gov.au)) has practical guides on device safety in family violence situations

---

*A vault that leaks is worse than no vault. Do the five-minute setup: disk encryption, fresh passwords, deliberate sync choice, one backup.*
