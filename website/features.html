What you can use today

From scattered records to facts you can trace.

Case Forge helps you organise evidence, examine accounts and prepare drafts with their sources close at hand. The free toolkit is available now. The all-in-one desktop app is still in development.

Updated 15 September 2026 · Free toolkit v0.2.0

A working case folder

The toolkit gives you folders for originals and working notes, eight document templates, a timeline, an evidence matrix, people profiles and guides for reviewing documents. You can use the Markdown files yourself or work with an AI assistant that has access to your chosen folder.

  • Organise documents: keep originals, correspondence and analysis in a structure you can navigate.
  • Compare accounts: record claims, supporting passages, conflicting material and unanswered questions.
  • Prepare your next step: assemble a chronology, questions for your lawyer and source-linked working drafts.
  • Keep context: track people, communications, legal advice, costs and follow-up tasks.
  • Handle evidence carefully: use digital-evidence attribution, source-checking, privacy and backup guidance. The included Australian parenting-law reference reflects the post-2024 framework and links to official sources; check current law before relying on it.

These are templates and guided workflows. Your chosen AI performs any analysis, and you review the result. The toolkit does not automatically establish what happened.

Keep reviewed facts from drifting

A date or time should not change just because a draft is rewritten. The toolkit now includes a verified-fact registry: one master record for a statement, its supporting evidence and the review that confirmed its exact wording.

Example: you verify that a message records arrival at 4:37 pm. Reference that fact in your chronology and case summary, and both receive the same exact wording when you generate a draft. An AI rewrite to “around 4:30” is a change the checks can flag.

  • Propose: record an exact statement, source IDs, quotations and page or timestamp references.
  • Verify: a person reviews the evidence and records their name and reason. Matching a quote alone does not verify that an event happened.
  • Reuse: reference the fact’s ID across draft templates, then generate documents from its stored wording.
  • Review corrections: competing evidence becomes a conflict. An accepted correction creates a new version while retaining the old one.
  • Check: flag changed sources, altered exports, damaged fact locks, missing references and drafts that use an older version.

Available now through the toolkit command. It needs Node.js 18+ and can be used by you or an assistant with local folder access. Read _system/verified-facts.md in the download for the full workflow and examples. A dedicated app review screen is still to come.

Updates flow into new drafts when their reference templates are rendered. Existing exports remain snapshots; signed or filed documents are not silently rewritten. Ordinary Markdown editors display the reference until you render it.

File fingerprints help detect changes. They do not make evidence tamper-proof, authenticate a reviewer or replace checking the source. Quotes from PDFs, recordings and images require manual inspection in this first version.

The original fact-locking and propagation idea was proposed by @bambam624 in GitHub issue #1.

Start with one document

Download the current toolkit, read CASE-FORGE.md and preserve your original document. Build an attributed note, check it against the source, then choose whether a statement is ready for deliberate verification.

Already using Case Forge? The installer does not overwrite conflicting files. Keep your existing case and registry backed up, install this version into a separate folder, and compare the updated guidance and tools before bringing changes into your case. Do not replace your case with the empty template.

Case Forge supports organisation and preparation. It does not determine legal truth, provide representation or promise an outcome. Cloud AI processes the content you choose to share; storing your folder locally is a separate choice.
